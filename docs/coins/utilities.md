Here’s my **5-tier utility + impact ranking**. This is **not price prediction**. Tier 1 means “most important real-world/protocol utility,” not “will pump.”

I’m treating: **A = Vaulta**, **W = Wormhole**, **HYPER = Hyperlane**, **BR = Bedrock**, **PARTI = Particle Network**, **VVV = Venice Token**, **ZBT = Zerobase**. Vaulta is the EOS rebrand/token swap, while PARTI, SPK, WAL, VVV, BR, SIGN, and ZBT are newer/less battle-tested, so I rate them more cautiously. ([Vaulta][1])

## Utility tier 1: highest utility / strongest ecosystem impact

**AAVE, AKT, ARB, ATOM, FIL, GRT, HBAR, LINK, POL, RUNE, TAO, TIA, W, ZRO**

These are mostly core infra: lending, oracle, storage, indexing, L1/L2 scaling, cross-chain, compute/AI, modular data availability, and native cross-chain liquidity. Aave is a major non-custodial lending protocol; Chainlink is core oracle/cross-chain infrastructure; Filecoin and The Graph handle storage and blockchain data indexing; Arbitrum, Celestia, Polygon/POL, LayerZero, Wormhole, Cosmos, Hedera, THORChain, Akash, and Bittensor all sit closer to base infrastructure than “single app” tokens. ([aave.com][2])

## Utility tier 2: strong utility, but more competitive / narrower / less dominant

**ALGO, APT, COMP, COW, DYDX, EGLD, ENA, ENS, HYPER, INJ, JUP, MOVE, ORCA, RED, SCR, SPK, STORJ, T, TRB, WAL, YFI, ZK**

These have real utility, but I would not rank them as universally important as Tier 1. Examples: **COMP/YFI/DYDX/ENA/SPK** are DeFi-focused; **ENS** is important identity/name infrastructure but scoped mainly to naming; **RED/TRB** are oracle competitors; **STORJ/WAL** are storage plays; **MOVE/SCR/ZK** are L2/zk/Move infrastructure but in a crowded category; **HYPER** is useful interoperability infra but competes with LayerZero/Wormhole. ([Binance][3])

## Utility tier 3: real but niche / ecosystem-dependent utility

**A, ASTR, BR, CHZ, CVC, DEXE, DIA, IOTX, KAVA, LISTA, LUMIA, MOCA, PARTI, PHA, RLC, SCRT, SFP, SIGN, USUAL, VVV, XAI, XVS, YGG**

These are not useless, but their impact depends heavily on whether their specific ecosystem grows. Examples: **CHZ** depends on sports/fan-token adoption; **MOCA** depends on identity/gaming/Animoca network adoption; **PARTI** depends on chain abstraction adoption; **VVV** depends on Venice AI usage; **BR/LISTA/USUAL** depend on DeFi yield/stablecoin/RWA demand; **A/Vaulta** has legacy EOS infrastructure but its new Web3 banking direction still needs stronger proof of adoption. ([Moca Network][4])

## Utility tier 4: limited utility, legacy, weak token capture, or unclear impact

**AGLD, AIXBT, AWE, BEL, FIDA, KNC, MASK, MOVR, ONG, PUNDIX, RARE, STEEM, TWT, WAXP, ZBT**

These can still trade actively, but from a utility-impact perspective they are weaker. Some are old/legacy ecosystems, some are very niche, and some have unclear token value capture. **AIXBT** has attention as an AI/market-intelligence narrative, but the token’s deep utility impact is weaker than real infra like TAO/AKT. **AWE** is an AI/autonomous-worlds narrative; interesting, but still early and less proven than core infra. ([Binance TH][5])

## Utility tier 5: mostly meme / narrative utility

**SPX, TRUMP**

These are mainly attention/liquidity/community narrative tokens. They can move hard in price, but their **real protocol utility and long-term infrastructure impact** are much weaker than the other categories.

My strongest utility picks from your list: **LINK, AAVE, ARB, FIL, GRT, ATOM, POL, TIA, TAO, AKT, W, ZRO, RUNE**.
